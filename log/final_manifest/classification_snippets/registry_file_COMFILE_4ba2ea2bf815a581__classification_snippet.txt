1. INTRODUCTION
(1) This Decision relates to the obstruction of an inspection conducted pursuant to
Article 20 of Regulation (EC) No 1/2003. This obstruction consists of producing
books or other records related to the business, required by the Commission during
the inspection, in incomplete form. An employee of the inspected undertaking
deleted WhatsApp messages from his mobile phone after he was informed that he
was a target of the inspection. When requested by the Commission, the mobile phone
was submitted for inspection without informing the Commission of the deletion.

6.2. Application to this case
(57) The inspection decision was addressed to both IFF Inc. and IFF France, such that
both of them were legally required to submit to the inspection. IFF France is a
wholly owned subsidiary of IFF Inc. and both entities constitute a single economic
unit and therefore one undertaking within the meaning of Article 101 TFEU and
Regulation (EC) No 1/2003.
(58) The behaviour of the relevant employee, consisting in the deletion of information
during inspection, must be attributed to IFF Inc. and IFF France. The relevant
employee was employed by IFF France, [...] also make IFF Inc. directly liable for
this conduct36. Both entities are held jointly and severally liable due to their direct
involvement in the infringement through the behaviour of the relevant employee. IFF
Inc. is also held jointly and severally liable for the conduct of its subsidiary IFF
France as its parent company.
(59) Having regard to the body of evidence in the Commission’s file, the facts described
in Section 4, the clear and unequivocal acknowledgements by IFF of the facts and the
legal qualification thereof contained in Sections 4 and 5, it is appropriate to address
this Decision to the following legal entities:
(a) International Flavors & Fragrances Inc., as direct participant and parent
company of International Flavors & Fragrances IFF (France) SAS; and
(b) International Flavors & Fragrances IFF (France) SAS, as direct participant.
(60) IFF Inc. and IFF France clearly and unequivocally acknowledged liability for their
direct participation in the infringement. IFF Inc. clearly and unequivocally
acknowledged that it is jointly and severally liable for IFF France’s direct
participation in the infringement, as the ultimate parent company directly or
indirectly holding 100% of the shares in IFF France.

7. REMEDIES
7.1. Article 23(1)(c) of Regulation (EC) No 1/2003
(61) Pursuant to Article 23(1)(c) of Regulation (EC) No 1/2003, the Commission may by
decision impose a fine not exceeding 1% of the total turnover in the preceding
business year when undertakings, intentionally or negligently, produce the required
books or other records related to the business in incomplete form during inspections
under Article 20 of Regulation (EC) No 1/2003 or refuse to submit to inspections
ordered by a decision adopted pursuant to Article 20(4) of the same regulation.
(62) In this case, the Commission considers that, based on the facts described and the
assessment contained in this Decision, the infringement has been committed
intentionally.
(63) It is therefore appropriate for the Commission to impose a fine on IFF.

7.2. Setting of the fine
(64) In fixing the amount of a fine, pursuant to Article 23(3) of Regulation (EC)
No 1/2003, regard shall be had both to the gravity and to the duration of the
infringement.
(65) In assessing the gravity of the infringement, the Commission has regard to a number
of factors, such as the nature of the infringement, the intentionality of the conduct,
the type of information deleted, and the position held by the employee who
committed the deletion. It must be also borne in mind that the fine must have a
deterrent effect.
(66) Inspections enable the Commission to gather material information concerning
suspected infringements. The power to carry out inspections is one of the most
important powers of investigation at the disposal of the Commission to detect
infringements of Articles 101 and 102 TFEU. When the Commission requests access
to data during an inspection, it is indispensable that the Commission must be able to
examine the entirety of such data. Therefore, the data must be produced in complete
form and must not be tampered with or deleted during the inspection. It follows that
the production of the required business records in incomplete or tampered form by
the inspected undertaking is considered a very serious infringement by nature.
(67) The special nature of electronic records makes the risk of manipulation particularly
high. The Commission considers that any fine for tampering with business related
records should be sufficiently deterrent to ensure to it does not pay off for companies
to take the risk of a procedural fine to avoid potentially high fines for breaches of
substantial law. The fine should also be sufficiently deterrent for dissuading other
undertakings from attempting to tamper with business records during a Commission
inspection.
(68) The infringement was committed intentionally, and it was committed by a senior
employee of the inspected undertaking who was a target person of the Commission
investigation37.
(69) The deleted information was in scope of the inspection decision and relevant to the
investigation38. The fact that the Commission inspectors were able to restore most or
possibly all the deleted data relatively quickly does not alter the gravity of the
infringement.
(70) Based on the elements above, it is justified to impose a fine of EUR 31 800 000
which corresponds to 0.30% of IFF’s total turnover in 2023.
(71) Furthermore, account must be taken of the following other factors relating to the
particular circumstances of the case.
(72) After the detection of the deletion of the information by the Commission, IFF proactively cooperated with the Commission.
(73) IFF formally admitted the facts in the minutes reporting the incident and IFF helped
to timely restore the deleted information to its fullest ability through the backup
system.
(74) IFF engaged in a cooperation procedure where it acknowledged liability for the
infringement and accepted a maximum fine in a cooperation submission.
