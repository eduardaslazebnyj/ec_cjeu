The Association of Japanese Automobile Manufacturers (JAMA) and the
Association of Korean Automobile Manufacturers (KAMA) commit to achieve an
average target of 140g CO2/km for their fleet of new passenger cars sold in the EU
by the year 2009. The commitments will be jointly monitored by the Commission
and JAMA and KAMA. The Commission has announced that it will consider
introducing binding legislation in the case the target is not met.
The commitments do not impose a target on the individual manufacturers, but only
an average target for all JAMA and KAMA members. Individual JAMA and KAMA
members are thus free to apply more stringent as well as less stringent levels of
CO2 emissions provided the average target is met. The car manufacturers are free
to develop and introduce new CO2-efficient technologies independently and in
competition with each other. The monitoring reports will not refer to the individual
companies’ achievements in order not to distort competition between the members.
Like in the case of ACEA the European Commission takes a positive view of the
commitments made by JAMA and KAMA to voluntarily reduce CO2 emissions.
They do not, in the Commission’s view, restrict competition under Article 81(1)
which refers to agreements between companies. The Commission has therefore
sent administrative letters to JAMA and KAMA informing them that it will not take
any further action.
