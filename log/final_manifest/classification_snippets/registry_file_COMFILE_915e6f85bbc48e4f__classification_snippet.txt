Whereas:

1. INTRODUCTION
(1) This decision relates to an incomplete reply by Eurofield S.A.S. to a request for
information adopted by the Commission by decision of 16 October 2023 pursuant to
Article 18(3) of Regulation 1/2003 (“Article 18(3) request”),3 which constitutes a
breach of the Article 18(3) request. Pursuant to Article 23(1)(b) of
Regulation 1/2003, the Commission may impose a fine of up to 1% of the
undertaking’s total turnover in the preceding business year to sanction an incomplete
reply to a request for information adopted by the Commission pursuant to
Article 18(3) of Regulation 1/2003.

6. LIABILITY
(56) Section 6.1 below sets out the principles regarding the liability for the infringement
of the Article 18(3) request. Section 6.2 below applies these principles to the
undertakings subject to this decision.

13

6.1. Principles
(57) For the Commission to impose a fine on an undertaking that has committed an
infringement, it is sufficient that this infringement has been committed by a person
who is generally authorised to act on behalf of the undertaking.41
(58) Where several legal entities may be held liable for participation in an infringement of
one and the same undertaking, they must be regarded as jointly and severally liable
for that infringement.
(59) According to settled case law, a parent company that owns 100% or almost 100% of
a subsidiary has the ability to exercise decisive control over such subsidiary. In such
a case, there exists a rebuttable presumption that the parent also in fact exercises that
control without the need for the Commission to adduce further evidence on the actual
exercise of control (the parental liability presumption).42
6.2. Application to this case
(60) Eurofield was the addressee of the Article 18(3) request and thus under an obligation
to provide a complete reply pursuant to Article 18(3) of Regulation 1/2003.
(61) Eurofield’s conduct can be attributed to both Eurofield and Unanime Sport, which
was the ultimate parent of Eurofield at the time of the infringement. Therefore,
Eurofield and Unanime Sport are jointly and severally liable for Eurofield’s conduct.
(62) Having regard to the facts and the body of evidence described in Section 4 above, the
Parties’ clear and unequivocal acknowledgements in their cooperation submission of
the facts and the legal qualification thereof, the Commission imputes liability to the
following legal entities:
– Eurofield, which committed the infringing conduct; and
– Unanime Sport, in its capacity as the parent company of Eurofield at the time
of the infringement which, by virtue of its 100% shareholding, was in a
position to exercise decisive influence over the conduct of its subsidiary
(see paragraph (59) above).

7.1. Article 23(1)(b) of Regulation 1/2003
(64) Pursuant to Article 23(1)(b) of Regulation 1/2003, the Commission may by decision
impose on an undertaking a fine not exceeding 1% of its total turnover in the
preceding business year when the undertaking supplies, intentionally or negligently,
incomplete information in response to a Commission request for information made
by decision adopted pursuant to Article 18(3) of Regulation 1/2003.
(65) In this case, the Commission considers that, based on the facts and the body of
evidence described in Section 4 above and the legal assessment contained in
Section 5 above, the infringement by Eurofield was committed at least negligently
(see in particular paragraphs (54) and (55) above). It is therefore appropriate for the
Commission to impose a fine on Eurofield and Unanime Sport, its parent company at
the time of the infringement.
7.2. Setting of the fine
(66) In fixing the amount of a fine, pursuant to Article 23(3) of Regulation 1/2003, the
gravity and to the duration of the infringement shall be taken into consideration. In
assessing the gravity of the infringement, the Commission gauges the nature of the
infringement and the context in which the infringement occurred. It must also be
borne in mind that the fine must have a deterrent effect.
(67) In this case, the Commission considers that the infringement was committed at least
negligently, which is corroborated by the circumstances in which the infringement
occurred, as set out in paragraphs (14) to (31) above.
(68) Further, the Commission considers the infringement to be serious in nature since
requests for information are one of the main investigative tools used by the
Commission to gather information in antitrust investigations and their effectiveness
depends on the undertakings concerned providing complete and accurate responses.
Therefore, omitting several pieces of information that are within the scope of the
Commission’s request for information can seriously hamper the Commission’s
ability to effectively investigate alleged anti-competitive conduct. Consequently, any
fine for providing an incomplete reply to a request for information pursuant to
Article 18(3) of Regulation 1/2003 must be sufficiently deterrent to ensure that it
does not pay off for companies to take the risk of a procedural fine to avoid a
potentially high fine for having breached the substantive provisions of EU
competition law.
(69) Considering that the infringement was instantaneous, the Commission does not
further increase the fine on account of duration.
(70) Based on the elements above, the Commission considers it justified to impose a fine
of [EUR 200 000 – 300 000], which corresponds to 0.30% of the 2024 turnover of the
single economic entity that Eurofield belonged to at the time of the infringement.
(71) Furthermore, account must be taken of other factors relating to the particular
circumstances of the case.
(72) In this respect, and in line with what is set out in paragraph (8) above, the
Commission took into account that the Parties signalled at a very early stage in the
procedure that they were willing to engage in cooperation discussions with the
Commission and the fact that they submitted the omitted documents to the
Commission, albeit only after the Commission had requested the Parties to do so.
The Parties were able to do so because they had proactively contracted a third-party
provider prior to the first cooperation meeting to analyse the mobile phone from

which the messages were eventually retrieved. In addition, the Parties submitted one

(73) In light of these particular circumstances, the amount of the fine is reduced by 30%.

(74) On the basis of the elements set out above, the amount of the fine to be imposed
pursuant to Article 23(1)b of Regulation 1/2003 is EUR 171 994.
