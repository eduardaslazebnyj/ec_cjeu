The agreement consists of a ‘Patent Pool’ for patents covering applications of DVD
technology. A DVD is the same size as a compact disc, but with more than seven
times its capacity. By using compression technology, a DVD disc can store video
and audio signals, as well as computer data and software allowing a single DVD-
Video disc to hold a two-hour feature film. Certain holders of essential patents have
agreed to license their patents through a single non-exclusive and non-discriminatory
license programme to be administered by Toshiba Corporation of Tokyo, Japan.
The companies submitted the agreement to the Commission in May 1999. The
investigation carried out by the Commission’s competition services found that this
patent pool would help promote technical and economic progress by allowing quick
and efficient introduction of the DVD technology . It was also found that the
agreement does not contain unnecessary or excessive restrictions on competition.
Therefore, the Competition Directorate General considers that the pool has
beneficial effects for the consumer. An administrative ("comfort") letter under Article
81(3) of the EC Treaty has cleared the programme.
The DVD technology was jointly developed by a consortium of ten companies, which
also include Philips, Sony, Pionneer and Thomson. The first three have also pooled
their patents, while Thomson has apparently decided to licence its patents alone.
Further details of the notification of the DVD Licensing Programme were published in
the Official Journal of the European Communities on 27 August 1999 (OJ C 242,
27.8. 1999, p. 5).
